# Gun ports — the gunner stands in the slit

Amends **prototype §4.2**, whose station rule says a station "requires ... a traversable crew
path", without ever saying where the crew member ends up. It says so now: in the station.

Amends the walk-graph rules of **crew-access §2** and **standable-ground §2** by adding one
kind to each of their two predicates, and corrects one measured number quoted in
**hatches §1**.

References written **§n** point at the P0 prototype spec, **hatches §n**, **crew-access §n**
and **standable-ground §n** at those documents.

Code and tests cite this document as `gun-ports spec n.n`.

---

## 1. Every gunner in the game is standing on the roof

A station is solid, so `WalkGraph` gives its crew the first standable cell touching it, in
direction order. Measured across every shipped design:

| Design | Station | Where its gunner stands |
|---|---|---|
| `reaching gun` | (3,1,-5) | **(3,2,-5)** — on top of the gun, over the lane |
| `stone keep` | (2,1,0) | (2,1,1) — inside, and only because the storey above blocks the roof |
| `wood frame` | (1,1,0) | **(1,2,0)** — on the parapet |
| `wood frame` | (3,1,0) | **(3,2,0)** — on the parapet |
| `standardTurret` | (1,1,0), (3,1,0) | **(1,2,0), (3,2,0)** — both on the parapet |

Four of the five stand on top of the wall, in the open, above the block they are firing. One
stands inside, and it is an accident of geometry rather than a rule: the `stone keep` is two
storeys, so its parapet cell is a wall block and the interior cell wins by default.

This is wrong twice over. It is wrong as a picture — §4.2's gunner is behind a firing port,
not on a roof — and it is wrong as a rule, because it makes the crew's position a
side effect of which neighbour the direction loop reaches first.

**A station is a firing slit.** That is what §4.2 has been describing since P0: "crew at a
station can be killed **through the port** without destroying the block". A port is an
opening in a wall, the gunner is behind it, and the wall is what stops the round that kills
them.

## 2. The rule

### 2.1 A station is passable

Crew move into a station's cell, as they move into a hatch's. Those two kinds are the
openings; every other block is solid.

They are not the same opening, and the difference is the point of 2.5: a hatch is a hole,
and a hole is person-sized *and* round-sized. A slit is person-sized only.

### 2.2 A station carries its own footing

A live station's cell is standable with nothing underneath it, exactly as a hatch's is. The
emplacement is part of the block — a gun port is a floor, a wall and a slit, not a hole cut
in someone else's wall.

Without this the rule would unman the `reaching gun`, whose station is the last cell of a
five-voxel cantilever with open air below it. That design is what §7.2's guided first run
opens on, and a rule that puts a violation on it is a rule that is wrong.

### 2.3 A stack of gun ports is not a ladder

Vertical movement still needs a hatch at one end (crew-access, walk graph). A gunner steps
*into* a port from the storey it is on; nobody climbs a wall by its windows.

This is the one place the two openings visibly part company, and it is why the walk graph
now asks two questions where it asked one: passability and standability take both kinds,
vertical movement takes hatches alone.

### 2.4 The gunner's cell is the station's cell

`StationReadout.crewCell` is the station's own position, and a resupply trip starts and ends
there — the runner walks to the post, not to a cell beside it.

**`ViolationKind.StationNoCrewSpace` is deleted.** A live station is always standable under
2.2, so "nowhere for the gunner to stand" can no longer happen; a violation that cannot fire
is worse than no violation, and crew-access §2.4 deleted `NoHatch` on the same grounds. The
question that can still fail is whether the gunner can *reach* the post, and
`StationNoEntryPath` is that question.

### 2.5 It still stops a shot, and still carries its load

Both unchanged, and both for one reason: **the section is mostly intact.** A slit takes a
person's shoulders out of a wall, not the wall's cross-section.

That is the whole of the difference from hatches §3 and §5, which give a hatch
`hatchCapacityFactor` of the capacity and let a round pass straight through it. A hatch
earns both of those by being a hole. A station earns neither, and §4.2 has already said so
from the other end: a round that kills the gunner **does not destroy the block**. A block
that survives what killed the man behind it is not an opening the round went through.

If playtesting says a gun port ought to weaken the wall it is cut into, that is a dial of
the same shape as `hatchCapacityFactor` and it belongs in its own document, with the voxel
table hatches §3.2 sets the example for. This one adds no dial and moves no load factor.

### 2.6 A station on the ground floor is a way in

It follows from 2.1 and crew-access §2.2, which already counts a route "through passable
cells" and already makes a hatch in an outer wall a door. A gun port big enough to sit in is
big enough to climb through, and it is not a cheap door: it costs a firing arc.

## 3. What it moved, measured

Structurally, nothing. Load factor, cost and violation count are unchanged on every shipped
design — `reaching gun` 0.960, the other three 6.400 — which is 2.5 doing its job.

**Every gunner moved into their station** (the right-hand column of §1's table becomes the
middle one). Two round-trip times moved with them:

| Design | Station | Round trip before | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reaching gun` | (3,1,-5) | 9.0 s | 9.0 s |
| `stone keep` | (2,1,0) | 3.0 s | **4.0 s** |
| `wood frame` / `standardTurret` | (1,1,0) | 3.0 s | **2.0 s** |
| `wood frame` / `standardTurret` | (3,1,0) | 4.0 s | 4.0 s |

Both moves are the rule being honest. The `stone keep`'s gunner is now in the wall rather
than in the middle of the room, so their magazine is one step further away. The
`standardTurret`'s first gunner has their depot in the cell directly behind the port, so
their trip is **zero steps and pays handling only** — a magazine behind the gun is a real
lever, and §4.3's cook-off radius is the price it already carries.

**Hatches §1 quotes the pair of numbers this moves.** Its measurement — replacing
`standardTurret`'s hatch with a plain wood block "leaves both stations' depot round-trips at
exactly 3.0 s and 4.0 s" — reads **2.0 s and 4.0 s** from here on. The point that measurement
was making is untouched: the hatch is still inert in that design, and the replacement still
changes nothing.

**Entry cells rose by one per ground-floor station**, which is 2.6 arriving: `wood frame` and
`standardTurret` 8 → 10, `stone keep` 9 → 10, `reaching gun` 24 → 24 (its station is out over
the lane and not on the ground floor at all).

## 4. What does not change

- **The firing arc**, which is traced from the station block and never consulted the gunner's
  cell.
- **Killing crew through the port** (§4.2), which matches on the station's block index and
  not on where the gunner is standing.
- **Damage, fire, mass, cost and every joint**, per 2.5.
- **Hatches**, in full. §2 of that document stands; this one adds a second passable kind
  beside it and takes nothing away.
- **`StationNoEntryPath` and `StationNoDepotPath`**, which are the two station failures that
  were always the real ones.

## 5. Tests this document requires

- A station's cell is passable and standable, and standable with nothing underneath it
  (2.1, 2.2).
- A hatch is still the only kind that lets crew move vertically: a two-high station stack is
  not a ladder (2.3).
- The readout's crew cell is the station's own position, on a design whose station has a
  standable neighbour that would previously have won (2.4).
- Every shipped design's load factor, cost and violation count is unchanged by this document,
  asserted as the exact numbers of §3 (2.5).
- `standardTurret`'s two round trips are 2.0 s and 4.0 s, and the first is a zero-step path
  (§3).
