# Hatches — an opening, not a block

Adds the section the hatch never had. **Prototype §4.2** names a hatch only as the far end of
a station's crew path and **§3**'s scope table credits "hatch access" with supplying anti-blob
pressure; neither says what a hatch *is*. This document says, and adds one row to the §5 dial
table.

References written **§n** point at the P0 prototype spec, **solver §n** at
[`../docs/structural-solver.md`](../docs/structural-solver.md).

Code and tests cite this document as `hatches spec n.n`.

---

## 1. What was there, and what was missing

The build gave the hatch two jobs. The spec named the wrong one.

- **Named, and inert.** A station needs a traversable path to a hatch (§4.2), checked in the
  editor. Crew never walk it: they are a fixed pool reassigned between waves, and nobody
  enters or leaves during a run. Measured — replacing `standardTurret`'s hatch with a plain
  wood block leaves both stations' depot round-trips at exactly 3.0 s and 4.0 s. The design
  plays identically.
- **Unnamed, and load-bearing.** A hatch is the only solid block crew can pass through, and
  the only way to move vertically: a hatch column is a ladder. That governs every path in
  the game, including the §4.3 depot hauls the prototype's cost argument rests on. No spec
  said so.

And it was free. Block kind reaches the structural solver and the damage verbs nowhere
except for depot detonation, so a hatch carried load like frame, stopped a round like frame,
and cost the same 1 material as the wood block it replaced. A design could be a fully
armoured lump with some of its voxels tagged as doors. §3's anti-blob claim for hatch access
was worth nothing.

## 2. A hatch is an opening

Four properties, and the last two are new.

### 2.1 Crew pass through it

The only block kind they can. Everything else is solid.

### 2.2 It is the only way through a floor

A hatch column is a ladder, and vertical movement needs a hatch at one end.

**A bare hole is not a ladder**, and that is deliberate rather than an oversight. An empty
cell over a void has no floor to stand on, so a hole cut in a deck is a fall and not a stair.
It is written down here because a reader who cuts a stairwell and gets no route deserves to
find the rule rather than deduce it.

### 2.3 It bears almost no load

See §3. This is the point of the document.

### 2.4 It does not stop a shot

See §5.

## 3. Load: the rule, and why the factor is not zero

**Joints incident on a hatch carry `hatchCapacityFactor` of the capacity they would otherwise
have**, in tension, compression, shear and torsion alike, and support joints under a hatch
with them.

Either end being a hatch is enough. A joint is only as strong as its weaker side, which is
already how mixed-material interfaces work (solver, joint capacities). Two hatches meeting
apply the factor **once, not twice**: the opening is the weakness, and two openings against
each other are not twice as weak as one.

### 3.1 The factor must be greater than zero

"Bears no load" cannot be taken literally, and this is the whole subtlety of the rule.

- A joint whose factor reaches zero is **not weak, it is absent** — the graph builder skips
  it. Measured on a three-block wood column: severing one joint takes the graph from 3 joints
  to 2 and puts both blocks above it in the floating set. Floating blocks collapse. A
  zero-capacity hatch would fall out of every turret on the first solve.
- Even kept in the graph, it would not help. Every block carries six equilibrium rows
  (solver, equilibrium), so a hatch must balance its own weight through something. With all
  its capacities at zero there is nowhere to put that force and the load factor for **the
  whole design** goes to zero — not merely the hatch's neighbourhood. The heatmap that
  §1.1's readability claim depends on would go uniformly red.

So the rule is: **a hatch carries its own weight and very little else.**

### 3.2 What the factor buys, in voxels

A wood voxel weighs 5 and an intact wood joint carries 160 in compression, so an intact wood
joint holds 32 voxel-weights and a hatch joint holds `32 x factor`:

| `hatchCapacityFactor` | hatch joint holds | free-standing hatch stack stands to | blocks a hatch carries above it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.03 | 1 voxel | 1 | 0 — it can only just hold itself |
| **0.1** | **3 voxels** | **3** | **2** |
| 0.25 | 8 voxels | 8 | 7 |

**P0 ships 0.1**, because the sentence a tester can derive from it — *a doorway holds a lintel
but not a storey* — is the most legible of the three. It is a dial (§5) and it is expected to
move.

## 4. What stacking does

The four cases, which are the reason the factor is a fraction rather than a ban.

1. **A ladder shaft works, and barely notices.** Legitimate hatch stacks are short: one hatch
   pierces one floor, and you stack them only to get through a thick deck or to shaft down
   through a solid mass. Each hatch in a floor is face-adjacent to the four floor blocks it
   is a hole in, so its weight sheds sideways at once instead of accumulating down the
   column. This is the case that must not break, and at 0.1 it is not close to breaking.
2. **A free-standing hatch tower fails at a computable height.** Nothing to shed into, so the
   bottom joint carries the whole stack: four hatches is 20 against a capacity of 16 and it
   comes down; three stands. That is the same kind of number as the five-voxel cantilever the
   worked examples turn on, and it is arrived at the same way — from the material table, with
   no rule declaring it.
3. **A storey cannot stand on a doorway.** A hatch carries two wood voxels plus itself, so a
   lintel and one course over a door is sound and a full wall on it is not. The heatmap says
   so before wave 1, which is what §1.1 is for.
4. **The all-hatch blob folds on its own.** Armour a lump and tag its voxels as doors so crew
   can walk through, and the whole thing has a tenth of its capacity; it comes down at about
   three courses. This is §3's anti-blob claim for hatch access, finally true, and true
   without a rule that says "no blobs".

## 5. A hatch does not stop a shot

**A hatch is not a contact for the kinetic verb.** A solid round passes through it, spends
nothing on it, damages it not at all, and strikes whatever is behind it.

A hole is a hole: the same idea that takes the load out of a hatch takes the armour out of
it, and neither needs its own justification.

Three consequences, all intended:

- **A hatch on the lane face is a firing port into your own turret.** Where the doors go
  becomes a spatial decision with an enemy in it, which is what §4.2's arc rule already is
  for guns.
- **A shot that finds only hatches all the way through leaves by the far side** and hits
  nothing. A wasted round, which is the correct outcome and not a special case.
- **Fire is unchanged.** A hatch is made of a material and burns exactly like it. Wood doors
  in a wood frame carry fire, and that is the same rule everything else obeys.

A hatch is still destroyed by collapse and by fire, and is still repaired against the
blueprint like any other block.

## 7. Not fixed here: "crew cannot get in"

The `NoHatch` and `StationNoHatchPath` violations ask whether a block **tagged** `Hatch` is
reachable, not whether crew can reach the outside. Both are wrong, and this document does not
fix them:

- An open doorway — the hatch deleted, the cell left empty — still raises both, although the
  walk graph makes that cell passable and standable.
- The message is false anyway. Measured: with `standardTurret`'s hatch replaced by a wall,
  there is still a **six-cell route from on top of the back wall to each gunner's post**.
  Crew climb in over the wall, because every P0 turret is open-topped.

The honest fix is for the **pad surface to be standable**, so that "outside" exists in the
walk graph at all and access can be asked as a question about reachability instead of about a
tag. That is a change with its own risk: exterior routes would give runners paths around the
outside of a design and move every depot round-trip in §4.3 — including the severed-depot
fixture that §4.3's "severing a corridor silences a gun" claim rests on. It needs its own
document and its own measurements, and it must not ride along here.

## 8. What does not change

**Cost.** A hatch still costs its material and nothing more. The price of a door is now
structural and ballistic, paid where the door is, rather than a number on the bill — which is
the shape every other pressure in P0 has.
